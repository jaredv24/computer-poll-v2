How The Computer Poll Works

The Computer Poll ranks teams on what they have done this season, with no preseason priors, recruiting data or predictive power ratings. Version 2.0 keeps that philosophy but measures it differently. Every part of the score draws on one team rating, and one calibrated probability does most of the work.

WHAT CHANGED

From v1.0.0 to v2.0

  • RPI-style opponent win percentage is replaced by an iterative rating, so schedule strength reaches past opponents' opponents.
  • Strength of Record uses fitted win probabilities and a Top-25 benchmark team. It is now the core of the score.
  • Results, Road Performance and Strength of Schedule are no longer separate components. Record, location and schedule are each counted once, inside Strength of Record.
  • Quality Wins rewards elite wins more steeply than average ones.
  • Dominance becomes Game Control: margin against expectation, with a smooth cap instead of a hard 21-point cap.
  • Components are combined as z-scores rather than a mix of percentages and percentiles.
  • Each team gets a rank range from resampling, replacing the Early Data label.
  • Weights are validated against 2015–2025 results.
METHOD

The Four Steps

1RATING

Team Rating

Every team gets one rating, r, solved from wins and losses only. Opponent quality, schedule strength and win probabilities all come from this number. It replaces v1.0.0's adjusted opponent win percentage (70% OWP + 30% OOWP).

$$P(i \text{ beats } j) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(r_i - r_j + h\,\ell_{ij})}}$$

Here ℓ is +1 when i is home, −1 away and 0 at a neutral site. Ratings are found by maximum likelihood across every game, so a win over a team that beat good teams is worth more, however many steps removed. OWP and OOWP stop two levels in, so a 9-3 team from a weak conference looks about the same as a 9-3 team from a strong one.

Undefeated teams. A pure win/loss model pushes a 6-0 team toward infinity. Each team gets one ghost win and one ghost loss against a rating-0 team, the same correction the Colley Matrix uses. It adds no preseason information and fades as games accumulate.

Scale. FBS average is 0. A 1.0 gap means the stronger team wins about 73% of neutral-site games.

FCS opponents. All FCS teams are pooled into one rated team, fitted from every FBS-vs-FCS game including the previous three seasons. v1.0.0 gave FCS opponents a quality of zero.

2ODDS

Win Probabilities

For a benchmark team B playing opponent j, the chance of winning comes from a curve fitted to real results:

$$p_j = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-a\,(r_B - r_j) - h\,\ell_j}}, \qquad 0.01 \le p_j \le 0.99$$

Calibration. The slope a and home-field term h are fitted on 2015–2025 games, each predicted from ratings through the previous week only. The prototype fit is a = 0.81, h = 0.34, from 5,501 games.

Benchmark team. B has the rating of the current #25 team. Against an average FBS team, which v1.0.0 used, nearly every good record scores close to 1.0. The question v2.0 asks is the committee's: how hard would it be for a good team to post this record on this schedule?

What this fixes. v1.0.0 used 0.50 + 0.40 × (0.50 − opponent quality), which keeps p between 0.30 and 0.70. That under-credited elite wins and gave FCS opponents p ≈ 0.70. The fitted model gives a .500 FBS team at home an 83–89% chance against FCS.

3RÉSUMÉ

Components

Record, schedule and location are counted once, inside Strength of Record. The other three add only what it cannot see. Below, pj is the benchmark's chance of winning game j and dj = 1 − pj is that game's difficulty.

Strength of Record 55%

How unusual is this record for a Top-25 team playing this schedule?

The benchmark's win total WB follows a Poisson-binomial distribution, computed exactly. For a team with W wins:

$$S = P(W_B > W) + \tfrac{1}{2}P(W_B = W), \qquad \text{SOR} = \Phi^{-1}(1 - S)$$

0 is a typical Top-25 résumé. +2 means only about 2% of Top-25 teams would do as well. SOR absorbs v1.0.0's Results, Strength of Schedule and Road Performance.

Quality Wins 20%

Who did they beat?

$$\text{QW} = \sum_{j \in \text{wins}} d_j^{\,2}$$

Squaring makes the curve convex. A win the benchmark gets 30% of the time is worth 0.49, a coin-flip win 0.25, and a near-certain win almost nothing. No base credit per win, no division by games played, and no separate location or margin bonus.

Loss Quality 15%

Were the losses forgivable?

$$\text{LQ} = -\frac{1}{L}\sum_{j \in \text{losses}} p_j^{\,2}\ \ (L \ge 1), \qquad \text{LQ} = 0\ \ (L = 0)$$

The average badness of a team's losses. A road loss at a top-5 team costs very little, and a home loss to a team the benchmark beats 95% of the time costs about 0.90. SOR already counts losses, so LQ averages. A summed version correlated 0.96 with SOR in the prototype; the average cuts that to 0.72.

Game Control 10%

Did they win convincingly, given who they played?

$$g(m) = 21\tanh\!\left(\tfrac{m}{14}\right), \qquad \text{GC} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_j \Big[g(m_j) - \hat g(r_i - r_j + h\,\ell_j)\Big]$$

The soft cap keeps a 3-point and a 17-point win apart (4.4 vs 17.6) while flattening blowouts: 35 and 56 points score 20.7 and 21.0. The expected value ĝ is fitted on past games: ĝ(x) = 19.1 tanh(0.60x). This replaces Dominance, whose schedule adjustment moved scores only about ±1.5 points. Game Control is the only component that uses scores.

4SCORE

The Computer Poll Score

Each component becomes a z-score across FBS teams, then is weighted and summed. z-scores keep magnitude that percentiles flatten.

$$\text{Score} = 0.55\,z_{\text{SOR}} + 0.20\,z_{\text{QW}} + 0.15\,z_{\text{LQ}} + 0.10\,z_{\text{GC}}$$

It is displayed as 50 + 10 × Score.

Head-to-head tiebreak. If two teams played and their scores fall within half the median gap between adjacent teams in the top 40, the winner ranks ahead. The window is recomputed weekly, replacing v1.0.0's fixed 1.0-point window. Cycles (A beat B, B beat C, C beat A) are skipped.

Uncertainty. Each resample replays every game from its fitted probability and reruns all four steps. The published rank always comes from actual results; the range shows how much it could move.

BACKTEST

Does It Explain the Results?

Retrodictive accuracy is the share of games already played that the higher-ranked team won. v2.0 roughly matches the best simple methods and has the fewest severe misorderings. Its gains are mainly coherence: each factor is counted once and weighted on purpose.
